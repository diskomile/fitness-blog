When it comes to **best cardio for fat loss**, two popular options are often debated: Low-Intensity Steady-State (LISS) cardio and High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT). Both methods have their pros and cons, and the best choice for you depends on your fitness goals, preferences, and current fitness level. In this article, we'll delve into the world of **LISS vs HIIT** and explore the most effective way to optimize your workout routine for maximum **fat loss**.

## Introduction to LISS Cardio
LISS cardio involves performing low-intensity aerobic exercises, such as jogging, cycling, or swimming, at a steady pace for an extended period. This type of cardio is often recommended for beginners or those who are new to exercise, as it's easy on the joints and can be done for longer periods without feeling overwhelmed. However, when it comes to **best cardio for fat loss**, LISS may not be the most effective option. To learn more about the importance of proper form and technique during LISS cardio, consider reading our article on [best workout gloves - do you actually need them](/blog/best-workout-gloves-do-you-actually-need-them).

## Introduction to HIIT Workout
HIIT, on the other hand, involves short bursts of high-intensity exercise followed by brief periods of rest. This type of training has been shown to be highly effective for **fat loss**, as it increases excess post-exercise oxygen consumption (EPOC), also known as the afterburn effect. HIIT workouts are often shorter than LISS cardio sessions, but they can be more challenging and require a higher level of intensity. For those looking to take their HIIT workout to the next level, consider incorporating [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload-the-key-to-continuous-gains) into your routine.

## Benefits of HIIT for Fat Loss
One of the primary benefits of HIIT for **fat loss** is its ability to increase EPOC. This means that your body will continue to burn calories at an increased rate after your workout is complete, resulting in enhanced **fat loss**. Additionally, HIIT workouts have been shown to improve insulin sensitivity, reduce inflammation, and increase human growth hormone (HGH) production, all of which can contribute to a leaner, healthier physique. To maximize the benefits of HIIT, it's essential to [calculate your TDEE and macros](/blog/how-to-calculate-your-tdee-and-macros) to ensure you're fueling your body for optimal performance.

## Comparison of LISS and HIIT
So, how do LISS and HIIT compare when it comes to **best cardio for fat loss**? The following table summarizes the key differences between the two:

|  | LISS | HIIT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intensity | Low | High |
| Duration | Long | Short |
| EPOC | Low | High |
| Caloric Burn | Moderate | High |
| Joint Impact | Low | High |

As you can see, HIIT is the clear winner when it comes to **best cardio for fat loss**. However, it's essential to note that LISS cardio still has its benefits, particularly for those who are new to exercise or prefer lower-impact activities.

* Q: How often should I do cardio to lose weight?
A: The frequency of cardio workouts depends on your fitness goals and current fitness level. Aim to do at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity cardio per week.
* Q: Can I do LISS cardio and still lose weight?
A: Yes, LISS cardio can still be effective for weight loss, particularly for those who are new to exercise or prefer lower-impact activities.
